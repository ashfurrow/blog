A blurry photo is caused by two things: a slow enough shutter speed, and motion. Either the camera moves or the subject moves. Today we’re looking at subject movement.

Not all subjects in a photo have to move; try to tell the tourists apart from the locals:

![Black-and-white view of a vast, arched station hall with tall windows, where blurred commuters cross the floor around a central information booth with a clock.](01.jpg#wide)

![Black-and-white photo of a blurred subway train passing a platform, with dark steel support columns in the foreground.](02.jpg#wide)

![Black-and-white street scene of a blurred cyclist in a helmet riding past benches on a city sidewalk, with a brick building behind.](03.jpg#wide)

I love blurry things, like I love film. Today’s photography world is far too focused on megapixels and clarity and sharpness; like film, blur is a reminder that none of that matters. Just shoot what feels right.

![Black-and-white photo of horizontal streaks of light and shadow, blurred as if taken from a moving vehicle.](04.jpg#wide)
